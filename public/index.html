1. Ask Jev how a request should be treated

Type what the request is for. Jev returns a typed priority with probabilities and whether it is safe to run twice. Confidence below 0.6 collapses to standard; hedging needs safe-to-retry of at least 0.7 and an idempotency key.

2. Put it under load

Two streams hit the guarded endpoint from your browser. Turn up failures or latency on the simulated upstream and watch the tier climb one step per 5 s window, the breaker open, then recovery walk back down.

3. Batch work yields to critical traffic

Submit a batch job; Jev scores how deferrable it is. While it runs, start the checkout stream at a high rate: the job is preempted at the next chunk boundary and resumes from its saved cursor once pressure clears. It never loses work and an aging guard stops it from starving.

Lifecycle: queued (Jev scores deferability) → started when pressure is clear → preempted at a chunk boundary when the tier is HARD_THROTTLE or above, the critical pool dips under its reserve, or a critical request raises the yield flag → waiting with the reason → aging-chunk after 10 s without progress under pressure → resumed from the saved cursor → done. Try: submit a job, then run the checkout stream at 15/s.
